# Maison Film Production Blueprint

## Film identity

**Working title:** *Time, Observed with Care*  
**Duration and format:** 30 seconds, 16:9, high-resolution editorial master.  
**Purpose:** A cinematic archive prelude for the Royal Horology Collection website and a premium social cutdown source.  
**Narrative posture:** The film does not assert private ownership, commercial availability, acquisition, or valuation. It presents a public editorial archive of horological appearances with restraint, attribution, and care.

> The narrator is an original neutral luxury voice. It does not imitate, recreate, or imply the voice of His Highness Sheikh Ammar bin Humaid Al Nuaimi.

## Global creative direction

The film is conceived as a **maison study in attention**, rather than a conventional product advertisement. Its visual language moves from darkness to parchment light, from a single glint of metal to an archive-wide constellation. The emotional effect should be contemplative, exacting, and quietly ceremonial.

| Dimension | Direction |
|---|---|
| Visual sub-genre | Contemporary haute-horlogerie editorial film, museum-caliber rather than retail-commercial |
| Image treatment | Photographic macro study, gentle film grain, considered negative space, no generated logos or text |
| Palette | Mineral ink, nocturne olive shadow, ivory light, burnished-bronze highlights |
| Motion | Slow dolly, deliberate rack focus, soft crossfades, no whip pans, no aggressive speed ramps |
| Recurring motif | A moving band of reflected light resembling a hand travelling around a dial |
| Narrator | Original smooth male documentary narrator, calm and unhurried; Arabic first, refined English closing |
| Music | Instrumental chamber-ambient score: felt piano, bowed-string texture, subtle metallic resonance, no vocals or percussion-led beat |

## Source and safety boundary

The only real-person material permitted in the film is the existing approved Royal Horology project imagery already used in the homepage cinematic sequence. Any new visual generated for the film must be abstract or anonymous: it may evoke craft, brass, light, archival paper, or watchmaking geometry, but must not fabricate a person, a specific watch, a brand mark, a private collection, or a factual event. The official gallery source path remains visible in the corresponding website context.

The delivered **33-second 16:9 H.264 master** is stored as `/manus-storage/royal-horology-maison-film-arabic_5df9808d.mp4` and is presented at the close of the homepage journey with fully bilingual contextual copy. It combines one anonymous, generated bronze macro movement with the approved 25-second Royal Horology cinematic sequence. Two original Modern Standard Arabic narrator spans are mixed at measured editorial intervals; no real-person voice is sampled, imitated, or synthesized.

The English narrator service did not complete after three original-voice attempts, and the original-score generation was also unavailable. ## Instrumental score blueprint

**Global music direction:** Instrumental only, no vocals. 30 seconds at 60 BPM in D minor. Contemporary chamber ambient with felt piano, restrained bowed strings, a low airy pad, and occasional fine metallic resonance. Avoid drums, percussion, crescendos, melodrama, Middle Eastern cliché instrumentation, and retail-advertising energy. Production is intimate, high-fidelity, spacious, and naturally reverberant.

| Time | Mood and arrangement | Intensity |
|---|---|---:|
| 00:00–00:06 | Sparse, private, and anticipatory: one felt-piano note family, distant room air, a single metallic resonance | 1/10 |
| 00:06–00:14 | Warm focus: piano chords become clearer, a barely audible bowed-string veil appears, with generous silence between gestures | 3/10 |
| 00:14–00:22 | Quiet elevation: strings widen in stereo, muted metallic harmonics trace the constellation motion, no rhythmic pulse | 4/10 |
| 00:22–00:30 | Respectful resolution: remove the pad, reduce strings to a sustained line, let piano and resonance fade naturally into silence | 2/10 |

## Production acceptance criteria

The finished film succeeds only if it keeps the real source imagery unaltered, keeps all newly generated visuals anonymous and abstract, uses no imitation voice, has no visible brand or product text generated by the model, and leaves the Arabic narration clear above the score. The website version may use a concise title overlay supplied by the web interface; the rendered master itself carries no on-screen text.
